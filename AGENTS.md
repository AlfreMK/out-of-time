# Out of Time: context for agents

Read this first when picking the project up in a new chat or with another agent. It captures what has been decided so far, so the user doesn't have to explain it again. `README.md` is the player/developer-facing overview; this file covers intent, preferences, decisions and gotchas.

## Working with the user

- **Chat in Spanish**, but **everything in the repo is American English**: code, comments, dialogue, README, commit messages. The game's Spanish translation lives only in `src/i18n/es/`.
- Stack is fixed: **TypeScript + Three.js + Vite**, no game framework. Keep dependencies minimal (runtime: only `three`). Follow the org rules: no secrets, no hardcoded credentials, safe defaults, suggest `npm audit`/Dependabot before deploying.
- **Historical and scientific accuracy is a top priority.** Every fact in dialogue, item names and visuals should be checkable (see "Accuracy decisions" below). When fiction is needed (Pike, Chronos, the time machine), keep it plausible and don't contradict known history.
- **Only the Araucanía era is set in Chile.** The other eras are elsewhere (Europe, Asia).
- The user plays the game and reports issues with short messages. They like things to feel "real": visible archers, believable ledges, longer distances, more to do. Nature should look natural, not man-made: no straight rows of trees or neatly lined-up stones (Hell Creek's stepping stones are loose river boulders, the Anzu's clearing is an uneven thicket, and each conifer is offset and sized a little differently within its tile). Hell Creek's big tree ferns (`f`) are walkable cover like the small undergrowth (solid ones looked like the small ferns and blocked the way); where the way must stay blocked (the Anzu's thicket) use conifers (`t`).
- Rejected ideas: **no noclip / walking through walls**, even in debug mode. A **Triceratops herd** in the Hell Creek meadow was tried (first as cover, then blocking sight, then scaring off a lookout raptor) and removed: it read as decoration and confused the user. The southern meadow was then reworked with the user: no raptors there anymore, but life that reacts to the player (an Anzu on its nest guarding the fern, a flock of birds that takes off loudly, *Thescelosaurus* that bolt unless you sneak) plus clearly-background life (hadrosaurs wading in the river, a *Champsosaurus*, dragonflies, pterosaurs gliding overhead with their shadows, frogs and katydids in the music).
- **Named arguments for new code** (the TypeScript take on Python's keyword-only arguments, `def f(*, a, b)`): a function or method that takes more than one argument takes a single object instead, destructured in the signature, so every call site names its values: `translate({ dict, text })`, not `translate(dict, text)`. One-argument functions stay positional. This doesn't mean every field needs a default. It applies to code written from now on (the i18n module follows it); older code is left as it is unless it's being reworked anyway.
- **No commits yet.** Ask before committing; if on `main`, branch first.
- **Dual license** (made on the user's own time): code under MIT (`LICENSE`), creative content (story, characters, player-facing text, level layouts, the Spanish translation) under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (`LICENSE-CONTENT.md`, which lists what counts). New characters or eras fall under the content license; keep `LICENSE-CONTENT.md`'s character list current. The build writes Three.js's MIT notice to `dist/third-party-licenses.md` (`build.license` in `vite.config.ts`), since the minifier drops its header.

## The game

A small third-person stealth adventure with a cel-shaded, chunky low-poly look (inspired by *Pizza Possum*/*Sifu*), a high follow camera, and no combat. Enemies are moving puzzles with vision cones, hearing and speech-bubble barks.

**Story.** Dr. Andrew Ward of the Chronos Institute (Geneva, 2026) runs Test Run #47 and gets stranded in time. Each repair raises the machine's stability and opens the next "window". He uncovers that Test Run #12 (Dr. Aaron Pike, October 2019) was secretly manned.

**Ending (a cliffhanger, `src/scenes/ending.ts`).** Pike betrays Andrew during the jump home: he has seen Chronos Corp rule 2087 and read in the Madrid museum that the Chronos Leaks bring it down, so he wants to *be* Chronos, and Andrew (who promised Yuki to tell everyone what Chronos did) is the only witness. He opens the passenger coupling and Andrew falls out of the field. In the Geneva lab, 60 seconds after departure, Pike steps out alone; Nora (his colleague) presses him about Andrew, Pike deletes #47's telemetry, field logs and reports (the wiped file Yuki finds in 2087) and leaves to "tell the director Aaron Pike is home"; Nora restores only the launch log to a forgotten backup partition (the one record that survives to 2087). Andrew wakes on a starlit plain in a year nobody knows yet (keep it unidentifiable: no landmarks, flora or fauna that date it), with no machine, core or Pip. Then "TO BE CONTINUED" and the credits ("Andrew Ward will return."). Foreshadowing in Madrid: Pike's journal ends with "Jump 4: Tokyo, 2087 ... It should have been mine" and torn-out pages, he asks whose cyberdeck is in the showcase (Andrew names Yuki), the showcase label says Chronos Corp's founder was never publicly named, and he climbs into the machine at `goHome()`. The user picked this over the old happy ending.

**Final puzzle.** The player must enter the origin year **2026** (`HOME_YEAR` in `src/eras/shared.ts`). The clues: in 2087 Yuki's leaked Chronos file says #47 launched 61 years earlier (everything after the jump was wiped, by Pike in the ending, so nobody knows what happened to Andrew; Yuki's Pike lines depend on whether Andrew has the recorder / saw the crypt panel); Pike says #47 was scheduled 7 years after his run in 2019. (Nora only appears in the 2026 lab: the user found an aged Nora in 2087 odd, so she was replaced by Yuki.)

## Accuracy decisions (keep consistent)

- 1248 forest: a lord's *Bannforst* (hunting reserve) where commoners risk being taken for poachers; wild boars rest in thickets by day; charcoal came from earth-covered kilns in the woods; water mills were common on Rhineland streams; wattle fences around village yards.
- Cretaceous: no grass (cover = ferns/horsetails), resin is not yet amber, *Dakotaraptor* is feathered and ~4.5–6 m (rendered at 1.6×), Pachycephalosaurus dome-butting is "debated", Chicxulub is ~0.5 Myr later, Quetzalcoatlus-like pterosaurs in the intro (and gliding over the map). South meadow: *Anzu wyliei* (Hell Creek oviraptorosaur, ~3.5 m, crest, toothless beak; brooding pose and paired eggs in a ring come from its relatives, e.g. *Citipati* fossils sitting on nests in Mongolia; blue-green eggs from *Heyuannia* in China, so the dialogue says "a relative"); dromaeosaur tracks are two-toed (the sickle claw was held up), so the meadow's three-toed tracks aren't a raptor's; *Thescelosaurus* ~3–4 m ("Willo"'s "fossil heart" was later judged an ironstone concretion); *Edmontosaurus annectens* up to ~12 m, no bony crest, dental batteries; *Champsosaurus* is a choristodere, not a crocodile, and survived the K–Pg extinction; some Cretaceous birds still had teeth; a grazing *Ankylosaurus magniventris* (solid decor) stands in the southwest corner. **No sauropods in Hell Creek**: the only late-Maastrichtian one in North America, *Alamosaurus*, lived far to the south (Texas, New Mexico, Utah), so the user picked the Ankylosaurus instead.
- 1248: old cathedral burned in April, foundation stone in August; Albert arrived in Cologne with Thomas that year; crossbows, heater shields, charcoal (not mineral coal), rye bread, a **bell-founder** casts bronze. Villagers speak "Middle High German" rendered by Andrew's **translator earpiece** as German-accented English ("zis", "ze"); guards' and the forester's barks and shouts stay in (modern, readable) German, like the Spanish soldiers' in Araucanía.
- Guard chats: 1248 (old cathedral fire in April, first stone in August, Albert writing on minerals, students calling Thomas "the Dumb Ox"); 1553, in Spanish (Valdivia in Concepción, the Quilacoya gold placers, December is summer, Extremadura, Lautaro the former groom).
- 1553: Lautaro served Valdivia as a groom; Battle of Tucapel on Dec 25, 1553 (Andrew deliberately doesn't spoil it); rukas face east; foye = *Drimys winteri* (John Winter, 1578, scurvy); Spanish soldiers with morions, cuirasses, arquebuses, Cross of Burgundy; Spanish barks stay in Spanish; Mapudungun words (*mari mari, peñi, chaltu may, lof, lawen, weichafe, wingka, pewkallal*).
- 2087: real Yamanote trains come every few minutes; the game compresses that to a 30 s loop (`game/train.ts`). The approach chime is the two-note "pin-pon" of Japanese announcements; wheels go "gatan-goton".
- 2087: the plaza's only rail is the Yamanote Line (a Tokyo Metro entrance was removed: an underground line next to the elevated one confused the user). Its station hall stands against the viaduct with a lit JR sign (山手線 / Yamanote Line) leaning toward the camera; trains pass through (the platforms are off-screen). As a train comes: the "pin-pon" chime, the station's announcement in a bubble ("Mamonaku, densha ga mairimasu", "Kiiroi sen no uchigawa made osagari kudasai", then the earpiece's English), an original platform melody (real JR melodies are copyrighted) and the train's electronic horn. The line color is uguisu (bush-warbler green).
- 2087: the Yamanote loop was completed in November 1925 (Kanda–Ueno link); its trains are silver with a yellow-green stripe; ema are wooden wish plaques at Shinto shrines.
- 2087 geography and logic: the plaza is Shibuya (Hachikō's statue, Shibuya-style crossing, Yamanote Line) and the canal is the Shibuya River in its concrete channel (not the Sumida, which is in east Tokyo). Everything north of the canal is Chronos Corp's restricted company district ("staff and permit holders only"): it has shops for the staff, the courier gets in on a delivery permit, the drones bark about trespassers ("UNAUTHORIZED PERSON?", plus "UNREGISTERED BIO-PRINT?" when Pip is along), and the big ads face the canal to shout at the crowds in the plaza. Ads are for invented products only (real brands are trademarks); their Japanese should read naturally, and the long-vowel mark ー is turned upright in vertical signs.
- 2240 exhibits: ammonites died out with the non-avian dinosaurs (66 Ma); trilobites lasted ~270 Myr until the end-Permian; the Iberian lynx fell below 100 in 2002 and recovered to the thousands by the 2020s; the last accepted dodo sighting was 1662.
- 2240: quartz is piezoelectric (quartz oscillators keep time in watches); most Madrid Metro stations lie some 15–20 m underground, cooler than the surface.
- 2087/2240: speculative but grounded in real tech (optical lattice clocks, REBCO, solid-state batteries) and real climate projections (drought and desertification risk in central Spain).

## Architecture

```
src/engine/    screen (WebGL + 2D UI layer at logical 320x180), input (keys, gamepad, glyphs, cheats), audio (procedural music themes + SFX), text
src/game/      game loop & fades, flow (scene transitions), world (the playable-era Scene: systems, HUD, pause, journal, debug),
               entities/ (state only: player, watcher, props), tilemap (markers, collision, ledges, LOS), state (save v2), ui
src/render/    three.js views: materials (toon, outline, silhouette), primitives, humans, creatures, machines, props3d, terrain (instanced), world-view, sets (cinematics)
src/eras/      one file per era + *-map.ts ASCII maps, types.ts (WorldApi/EraDef), shared.ts (machine menu, final puzzle), info.ts
src/i18n/      languages: tr/t/msg/names, keys.ts (generated), messages.ts (text built from values), es/ (Spanish dictionaries)
src/scenes/    title, intro (3D cinematic), travel (tunnel), ending (betrayal in the tunnel, Geneva lab, stranded Andrew, "to be continued", credits with accuracy notes)
scripts/       validate-maps.ts and i18n.ts (run in Node with type stripping; node.d.ts types the few Node APIs they use)
```

Story flags come from the `Flag` const object in `src/game/flags.ts` (`w.flag(Flag.AraTrunk)`), and per-era/per-item flags from its builders (`progress.fixed(era)`, `progress.visited(era)`, `progress.diagnosed(era)`, `progress.got(item)`). `flag()`, `setFlag()`, `once` and `openFlag` only accept `FlagName`, so add new flags to `Flag` first; the saved string values must not change (old saves depend on them).

Dialogue speakers come from the `Speaker` const object in `src/game/speakers.ts` (`[Speaker.Andrew, '...']`); `Line` only accepts those names, so add new characters there first. NPC `name`s use it too.

Key rule: **entities hold state, render/ draws it**, and **era scripts only use `WorldApi`** (no DOM imports), which is why `npm run validate` can run them in Node.

### Systems worth knowing

- **Languages** (`src/i18n/`): English (default) and Spanish, picked in **Settings** (title screen and pause menu, with US/Chile flags; Settings also holds the sound toggle) and kept in `localStorage['out-of-time.lang']`. Everything is checked by types: (1) English text written in the source stays there, `npm run i18n` collects it into the `…Text` unions of `keys.ts` (one per dictionary: each era, scenes, ui), and every dictionary is `satisfies Record<…Text, string>`, so a missing, misspelled or stale entry fails `typecheck` (a one-character change in the English too); `validate` fails if `keys.ts` is out of date. Era scripts keep passing plain English, but every place that takes text for the screen is typed `Text` (barks, chats, inspect/obstacle labels) or `ScreenText` = `Text | Shown` (dialogue `Line`s, toasts, menus, objectives), so English that isn't in `keys.ts` is a type error right where it's written (change a bark by one character and the editor flags it; `npm run i18n` then moves the error to the dictionaries). Local lists of text need a type (`const steps: Text[]`, `satisfies Partial<Barks>`), or they widen to `string`. `tr()` translates it where it reaches the screen (dialogue box, bubbles, prompts via `Entity.promptLabel()`, toasts, objectives, menus), and code naming a text uses `t()`. `Shown` is text already in the player's language: what `msg()` builds, or `verbatim()` for names already translated or text with no language (glitched readouts); `isText()` tells keys from other strings at runtime (button glyphs). A speechless `'...'` line counts as text. (2) Text built from values is never a template literal outside `src/i18n/` (`validate` rejects it): it's a function in `messages.ts` with named arguments (`msg().gotItem({ item })`), which `es/messages.ts` implements with the same type. (3) Item, character and era names are translated by id: `itemName()`, `itemList()`, `speakerName()`, `eraInfo()` (`es/names.ts`, `Record<ItemId | SpeakerName | EraId, …>`). The journal stores the English and translates on display, so switching language translates past lines too (text from `msg()` is stored as shown). The dialogue box pages a line that doesn't fit in 3 rows; HUD panels size to their text. Style and glossary: `src/i18n/README.md` (neutral Latin American Spanish; Cologne villagers speak German-accented Spanish; German and Spanish barks, romaji and Mapudungun stay as they are).

- **Maps**: ASCII rows; lowercase/symbols are tiles (per-era `TileSet` in `tiledefs.ts`), uppercase/digits are markers (`*_MARKER_BASE` gives the tile under each). A marker used twice is an area (bounding box). `TileDef` flags: `solid, hide, noise, dark, low, seeThrough, ledge (one-way direction), height (visual only)`.
- **Watchers** (`entities/watcher.ts`): kinds raptor/anzu/guard/soldier/rider/dog/camera/drone/bot; patrol via A* (`pathfinding.ts`: 8-way, no corner cutting, then `smoothPath` into straight lines) between route stops (`route` is a marker string, or a list mixing markers and `[tx, ty]` tiles for maps with no markers left); walking to a noise or back to the post adds a cost near the player, so they detour instead of walking right past the thrower; suspicion meter; `hear()` with per-kind hearing and a `NoiseKind` (`step`, `noise` = pebbles/glass/puddles/`w.alarm`, `horn` = an ally's trutruka or a flock taking off, `food`); barks; `group` + `w.disable(group, s)` for hacking. `posted: true` sentries ignore `noise` (bark + `onHoldPost`), only turn their head at steps and only leave for a `horn`; the validator requires an always-present ally within 150 px (or a flock within its racket's reach) of each one. `Watcher.idle` (standing at a stop, nothing going on) lets the brooding Anzu settle on its nest.
- **Chats** (`entities/chat.ts`, `w.chat({ between: [id, id], talks, distracted })`): two watchers with `id`s talk whenever both stand still within 48 px (a patrol stop next to a post holds until the talk ends). `distracted` turns them toward each other with cones eased to 60% range / 50% fov (`viewRange`/`viewFov`; `isSafeSpot` keeps using the full `range`); posted sentries use `distracted: false`. Any suspicion or noise breaks it off; 10 s cooldown. Long bubbles wrap to two rows. Pairs: Cologne yard patrol + keep door guard (accented English), Araucanía gate sentries (Spanish).
- **Train volume**: the train's sounds scale with distance from the station (`trainNearness()`: full by the viaduct, 30% from the avenue and the depot, where it still covers splashes). `audio.sfx(name, volume)` plays any effect quieter.
- **Train** (`EraDef.train`, `game/train.ts`): a fixed timetable on the World clock (away 14 s, approaching 6 s, passing 10 s). The 3D train in `terrain.ts` reads the same function. While passing, `World.noise()` drops `step`/`noise` (rings drawn faint). Audio: `chime` on approach, `jingle` 1.4 s later, `trainhorn` as it rolls in, a rumble bed driven by `audio.train(level)` (silenced on pause and scene change), `clack`s. HUD board under the era label.
- **Items**: Minecraft-style bar at the bottom center (`drawHotbar`): number keys, Q, mouse wheel, LB/RB (L1/R1, also Y) or a tap pick a slot (`Input.hotbar` + the `.touch-hotbar` strip in `touch.ts`). Pebbles/bread: tap = 56 px throw, hold winds up to 136 px over 0.8 s with a landing marker.
- **Hazards**: `rocks` (falling, shield protects) and `arrows` (sideways, or from `shooters` markers = the archers on medieval balconies).
- **Era API helpers**: `inspectEach(marker, label, (w, i) => …)` and `decorEach(marker, kinds)` act on every occurrence of a marker in reading order (the mineral hall's five showcases share `9`); `alarm(radius)` makes a noise at the player.
- **Allies + pifilka**, **gates** (laser/door/palisade with `openFlag`), **decor** (statues, flags, archers, whale, river animals), **companion** (Pip follows the player's trail).
- **Signs** (`w.sign`, `Sign` in `props.ts`, artwork in `render/signs.ts`): Neo-Tokyo's ads, as `screen` (on a facade, cycling its `ads` with a static glitch), `kanban` (vertical neon, tategaki) or `rooftop` (billboard on posts with chasing bulbs). They lean back to read from the high camera, must fit under the building's roofline (heights come from `2.6 + hash2(tx, ty, 1) * 2.2` in `terrain.ts`), and `hideIndoors` hides one whose wall drops away while Andrew is inside. Tiles carrying a sign get no random kanban or rooftop clutter from the terrain (it poked through them); `inset: 0.5` mounts one on a pole billboard (`h`). The validator checks each sign is on a building tile.
- **Flocks** (`w.flock`, `Flock` in `props.ts`): birds pecking on the ground; walking (not sneaking) within 26 px sends them off with a `horn`-kind noise of `FLOCK_NOISE` px, so even `posted` watchers leave to look; they land again after 14 s, but not while a watcher on the lookout or Andrew is within `FLOCK_CLEAR` px (so they don't land at the feet of the Anzu that came to check on them). The validator counts flocks like allies for posted watchers. **Critters** (`w.critter`, `Critter`): harmless solid animals that graze near their spot and bolt from a walking player (sneak to get close and inspect them); they never step onto the player, so they can't trap him.
- **Cutaway walls** (`terrain.ts`): wall tiles 1–2 rows south of an interior floor (`INTERIOR_LOOKS`) go in one group per floor look, which sinks only while the player stands on that floor (e.g. Pike's lab wall stays up while you walk the museum gallery, and sinks when you step onto the lab floor).
- **Camera pans**: `w.lookAt(point)` glides the camera over to a spot while a script talks about it, `w.lookAt(null)` follows Andrew again (reset on respawn). The Hell Creek nest hint uses it: its trigger ring sits close enough to spot the nest (validated), and the pan keeps the Anzu clear of the dialogue box, which covers the bottom third of the screen.
- **Music**: `EraDef.music`, `musicZones`, `darkMusic`; `w.music(theme|null)` overrides (alarm).
- **Saves**: `localStorage` key `out-of-time.save`, version 2 (items, flags, checkpoint, journal log), validated and migrated from v1. Checkpoints save on activation; Continue spawns at the last checkpoint.
- **Checkpoints**: besides each era's fixed `w.checkpoint(marker)`s, `World.isSafeSpot()` turns any quiet spot into one (every 48 px of walking): no watcher suspicious or searching, out of every watcher's vision range + 20 px (with line of sight) from anywhere on its patrol loop (`Watcher.patrolPoints()`), 80 px from sleepers, off noisy/ledge tiles, hazards and blocking triggers. So getting caught never sends the player back past a patrol they already passed (the user asked for this after Hell Creek).
- **Touch / mobile** (`engine/touch.ts`): DOM overlay with a floating joystick (left half; gentle drag = creep) and gamepad-style A/B/X/Y buttons plus Menu (pause + skip + back, like Esc) and fullscreen. `Input.device` becomes `'touch'` only from a real finger (or `(pointer: coarse) and (hover: none)` at load, i.e. phones/tablets); a key, a mouse click or a connected gamepad puts the overlay away. Releasing the stick must never set the device (it runs on window blur), or the overlay pops up on desktop. Portrait phones get a "turn sideways" notice; the 3D pixel ratio is capped at 1.5 on touch devices. The page must never zoom (a stuck zoom can't be pinched back): the viewport forbids scaling, and `preventZoom()` cancels a second touchend within 350 ms (iOS double-tap zoom), `gesturestart` and `dblclick`, as in the user's AlfreMK.github.io game.
- **UI**: dialogue box (records to the journal, skipping a line identical to the previous one; `{interact}`-style tokens become device glyphs), journal (hides back-to-back repeated blocks of up to 16 lines via `withoutRepeats()` in `state.ts`; Up/Down scroll and auto-repeat when held, Left/Right page, mouse wheel, scrollbar), auto-sizing choice menu, year picker, pause menu (goal from `EraDef.objective`, journal, settings, controls by device, inventory).

## Gotchas (learned the hard way)

- The shell is zsh with **`noclobber`** (use `>|` to overwrite), and **`rm`/`cp` are interactive aliases**: use `/bin/rm -f`, `/bin/cp -f`, or the command hangs (and can leave files half-edited while the user's dev server hot-reloads them).
- `tsconfig` uses `erasableSyntaxOnly`: no enums, no constructor parameter properties. Import local files **with the `.ts` extension**.
- A solid NPC on a 1-tile-wide doorway blocks the route (happened with Brother Thomas). Keep NPCs off chokepoints; the validator now catches it.
- Area-triggered cutscenes assume the player arrives from one side. Either enclose the area so there is a single entrance (the Mapuche camp has a rock ring, open only on its south path; validated), or move NPCs relative to the player (`moveBy` toward `w.player`) instead of to fixed markers.
- `Entity.height` is the character's size for overhead UI. Don't reuse the name in subclasses (the bolt uses `altitude`).
- Avoid coplanar faces in `terrain.ts` (z-fighting flickers, e.g. the brown house trim used to share the wall's top face): offset details slightly proud of the surface and inset overlapping blocks (cutaway stubs are 0.98 wide). The sun's shadow camera is snapped to whole texels (`world-view.ts`) so shadows don't shimmer as the camera moves.
- `MeshToonMaterial` has no `flatShading`; `pivot()` must not call `add()` with zero children.
- Map edits: keep row widths constant; markers replace tiles (set the base in `*_MARKER_BASE`). Markers are only `A-Z0-9`, and the medieval and Neo-Tokyo maps use all 36 (Hell Creek has only `0` free): use explicit `TileRect`s for trigger areas, and `[tx, ty]` route stops and inspect spots (`w.inspect([17, 36], ...)`, the Neo-Tokyo station) instead of new markers.
- The camera looks from the south at a high angle: a tall tile (tree, wall, roof) right south of something small hides it. Pickups (yellow glow + blue silhouette), the player (blue) and enemies (red silhouette, `addSilhouette()` in `entity-views.ts`) show through scenery, and vision cones draw on top, but keep patrol lanes readable anyway: no trees in the 2 rows right south of a patrol (the forester's ride has low blackberry brambles `v` instead).
- Attach every part of a model (e.g. Andrew's back shield) **before** `addSilhouette()`: a mesh added later keeps render order 0, draws before the ghosts, and the silhouette paints over it (the shield turned blue when walking north).
- Upright signs read almost edge-on from the high camera: lean them back toward it (the Madrid Metro rhombus is tilted −0.75 rad).
- Inspect spots (signs, panels, statues) answer on their whole tile (`findInteractTarget`); before, the prompt only showed up in a tiny circle.
- Neo-Tokyo: buildings in the map's bottom row and the railway viaduct are drawn low on purpose, so they don't hide the plaza from the camera.
- Noisy tiles sound the moment the player steps onto them (`Player`), not only on the footstep timer, which used to let a fast walk skip a single bone, puddle or glass tile.
- Headless testing: Chrome may see a gamepad attached to the Mac, and its stick overrides the keyboard. Stub it with `page.evaluateOnNewDocument(() => { navigator.getGamepads = () => []; })`.
